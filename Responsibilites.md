A **Solution Architect** is responsible for translating business needs into a secure, scalable, and cost-effective technical solution. They are the bridge between business stakeholders, developers, infrastructure teams, security teams, and operations.

For your course, I recommend teaching the Solution Architect role through the **Solution Architecture Lifecycle**, rather than just focusing on cloud services.

---

# End-to-End Responsibilities of a Solution Architect

## Phase 1: Understand the Business

### Responsibilities

* Meet stakeholders
* Understand business goals
* Identify business problems
* Gather functional requirements
* Gather non-functional requirements
* Understand budget and timeline
* Identify risks and constraints
* Define success criteria

### Deliverables

* Business Requirement Document (BRD)
* Stakeholder Map
* Vision Document
* Requirement Traceability Matrix

---

# Phase 2: Analyze the Existing Environment

### Responsibilities

* Review current applications
* Understand existing infrastructure
* Review integrations
* Review databases
* Analyze network topology
* Review security posture
* Identify technical debt

### Deliverables

* Current State Architecture
* Gap Analysis
* Risk Assessment

---

# Phase 3: Design the Solution

### Responsibilities

Choose:

* Application architecture
* Infrastructure
* Cloud services
* Databases
* APIs
* Integration methods
* Identity solution
* Security controls
* Disaster recovery strategy
* Scalability strategy

### Deliverables

* High-Level Design (HLD)
* Low-Level Design (LLD)
* Architecture Diagrams
* Technology Selection Document

---

# Phase 4: Technology Evaluation

### Responsibilities

Evaluate:

* Azure vs AWS vs GCP
* SQL vs NoSQL
* App Service vs AKS
* VM vs Containers
* Functions vs App Service
* Service Bus vs Event Grid
* Blob vs File Storage

Always explain:

* Why this option?
* Why not another option?
* Cost
* Performance
* Scalability
* Maintainability

# Soft Skill Responsibilities

A great Solution Architect spends a significant amount of time communicating.

They should:

* Lead architecture workshops.
* Facilitate requirement gathering sessions.
* Present solutions to executives.
* Explain technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders.
* Negotiate trade-offs between cost, performance, security, and delivery timelines.
* Mentor developers and junior architects.
* Coordinate across development, infrastructure, security, operations, and business teams.

## A Practical Way to Teach This

Rather than teaching isolated technologies, organize your course so that students **act as Solution Architects** for each project. In every project, require them to:

1. Understand the business problem.
2. Gather and validate requirements.
3. Design the architecture and justify technology choices.
4. Address security, scalability, cost, and operational concerns.
5. Produce professional documentation and diagrams.
6. Defend their decisions before an Architecture Review Board.

This mirrors the work performed by Solution Architects in enterprise consulting and product organizations, where success depends as much on sound decision-making and communication as on technical expertise.
